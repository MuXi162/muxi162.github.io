About Me

Honestly? My job is to make complex things make sense. Whether that's helping a business team understand what's actually possible with their data, or helping an engineering team understand what a business actually needs — I'm usually the person in the middle making sure everyone's speaking the same language.

I've spent my career in technical operations, working on everything from field workforce management to building the data foundations that let organizations think about AI seriously. Not "AI" as a buzzword, but actual, useful, agentic capabilities that make people's work better.

Right now I'm deep in the work of figuring out how fiber infrastructure companies can use their operational data more intelligently. How technicians close out jobs, how managers spot patterns they'd never see manually.

Outside of work I'm a dad, a casual golfer, and someone who builds side projects just to see what's possible. This website is one of them, built collaboratively with Claude AI as a way to learn and to have something to show beyond a resume bullet point.

How I Think

Data Maturity Is a Journey, Not a Product

One of the frameworks I keep coming back to is the idea that organizations don't jump straight to AI. They earn it. You have to build the foundation first, or everything you build on top of it wobbles.

I've used a bronze-silver-gold model to help teams understand where they actually are versus where they want to be. It sounds simple, but it's surprisingly hard to get leadership to admit they're at bronze when everyone assumed they were already at gold.

Once you're honest about that, the path forward gets a lot clearer and a lot more achievable.

Bronze

Data Foundation

Consistent, clean, reliable data at the source. Getting systems to agree on basic facts before anything else.

Silver

Integrated Analytics

Connected data across systems. Dashboards that tell a story. Teams that actually use the reports being built for them.

Gold

Agentic Intelligence

AI that acts on data in real time. Predictive signals. Systems that help people do their jobs better without getting in the way.
